# World Trade Center Evidence Engine

## Mission

Create the most historically accurate, transparent, and evidence-backed
digital reconstruction of the original World Trade Center complex.

The project exists to collect, preserve, organise, verify, connect, and
make accessible historical evidence concerning the original World Trade
Center.

The completed system will support both historical research and the
construction of an explorable digital twin.

## Core Principle

Evidence takes priority over assumptions.

Every reconstructed building, floor, room, structural element, object,
material, event, and spatial relationship should be linked to supporting
historical evidence wherever evidence is available.

When the available evidence is incomplete, conflicting, or uncertain,
the system must preserve and display that uncertainty rather than hide it.

## Scope

The project covers the original World Trade Center complex, including:

- Towers
- Buildings
- Plazas
- Public spaces
- Observation facilities
- Restaurants
- Offices
- Retail areas
- Transportation connections
- Structural systems
- Architectural details
- Construction history
- Operational history
- Changes over time

The project is not limited to the North Tower and South Tower.

## Automated Evidence Gathering

The finished engine must not depend on permanent manual uploading of
evidence.

The system must be capable of automatically:

1. Discovering relevant evidence from configured sources
2. Recording candidate URLs and source information
3. Downloading permitted evidence
4. Detecting duplicate URLs and duplicate files
5. Storing files and metadata
6. Registering assets in the database
7. Classifying and routing assets
8. Processing PDFs, images, drawings, video, audio, and records
9. Extracting structured knowledge
10. Preserving citations and provenance
11. Verifying facts and relationships
12. Updating the knowledge graph

Manual placement of files into incoming directories is a development and
testing mechanism. It is not the final evidence-acquisition workflow.

## Evidence and Provenance

Evidence records should preserve, where applicable:

- Source organisation
- Source title
- Original URL or archive reference
- Local or object-storage location
- File hash
- Publication or creation date
- Acquisition date
- Page number
- Drawing or sheet number
- Image identifier
- Video or audio timestamp
- Extraction method
- Confidence
- Verification status
- Rights or usage information

The engine must be able to explain where stored knowledge came from.

## Use of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence may assist with:

- Classification
- OCR interpretation
- Document summarisation
- Entity and fact extraction
- Relationship suggestions
- Relevance scoring
- Image and drawing interpretation
- Research assistance

Artificial intelligence must not become an uncited source of truth.

AI-generated conclusions must be distinguished from directly observed or
extracted evidence and must retain links to the evidence used to produce
them.

## Reconstruction Standards

A reconstruction element should be classified according to its evidence
status, such as:

- Verified
- Well supported
- Supported
- Provisional
- Inferred
- Disputed
- Unknown

The digital twin must make these distinctions visible to researchers and
users.

## Delivery Phases

### Phase 1: Evidence Engine

Build an automated, evidence-driven archive and knowledge system that can
discover, ingest, process, cite, verify, search, and connect historical
evidence.

### Phase 2: Knowledge Model

Represent the World Trade Center complex as connected buildings, towers,
floors, spaces, systems, events, assets, facts, sources, and
relationships.

### Phase 3: Reconstruction

Reconstruct selected areas using evidence-backed geometry, materials,
objects, photographs, drawings, and documented spatial relationships.

### Phase 4: Explorable Digital Twin

Create a historically grounded digital twin and walkthrough in which
reconstruction elements can be traced back to supporting evidence.

## Definition of Success

The project succeeds when a user can explore the original World Trade
Center complex, inspect the evidence supporting reconstructed elements,
understand the confidence and uncertainty attached to those elements,
and trace important claims back to their original sources.
