## 1. SETTING THE SCENE: DAY 1 (1966)

It is **August 5, 1966**.

You step into lower Manhattan at the intersection of Church Street and Cortlandt Street.

The air smells of diesel exhaust, fresh turned earth, salty harbor breeze from the nearby Hudson River, and roasting coffee beans from nearby downtown merchants. 

The Twin Towers do not exist yet. Neither does the 5-acre plaza, the marble concourse, nor the PATH train terminal.

In their place is **Radio Row**—a historic 16-acre neighborhood of 19th-century brick storefronts, electronic repair shops, overhead power lines, cobblestone streets, and active demolition and excavation sites. Wooden construction hoarding fences wrap around the perimeter, bearing large wooden Port Authority signs: **"FUTURE SITE OF THE WORLD TRADE CENTER — PORT AUTHORITY OF NY & NJ."**

## 2. THE FIRST 10 MINUTES: VISITOR TIMELINE

### Minute 0: Arrival & Atmosphere
- **Where You Stand:** You stand on the cobblestone sidewalk outside the Cortlandt Street station exit.
- **What You See:** A sprawling 16-acre panorama of demolition and groundbreaking. Wooden timber scaffolding, yellow Bucyrus-Erie cranes, Mack dump trucks, and temporary wooden pedestrian walkways. To the west, the Hudson River piers (Piers 13 and 14) are visible beyond West Street.
- **What You Hear:** The steady rhythm of diesel engines, steam shovels digging into Manhattan schist, workers calling out across the trench, distant harbor tugboat whistles, and the metallic clatter of drill rigs.
- **What Invites Exploration:** A wide wooden pedestrian ramp leading through a gap in the perimeter fence, marked with a hand-painted wooden sign: **"VISITOR PLATFORM & ARCHITECTURAL EXHIBIT ->"**.

---

### Minute 1: Walking Through Radio Row
- **What You See:** As you walk down the timber ramp into the site interior, you pass the remaining brick facades of Radio Row shops being carefully dismantled. 
- **The Ground Underfoot:** Gravel, crushed brick, and heavy timber matting covering mud.
- **Construction Activity:** Yellow bulldozer tractors clearing debris while survey crews with brass transits and wooden leveling rods mark the future core coordinates of Tower A (WTC 1) and Tower B (WTC 2). White chalk lines painted onto the exposed bedrock mark the 47 core column footings.
- **Worker Presence:** Construction workers wearing metal hard hats, leather work boots, and denim overalls guiding dump trucks loaded with excavated earth toward West Street.

---

### Minute 5: Stepping Into the Port Authority Site Trailer & Drawing Room
- **What You See:** You step off the dirt pathway and open the screen door of a green wooden Port Authority field office trailer. Inside, cool air blows from a window air conditioner, mixed with the smell of blueprint ammonia, pencil lead, and fresh coffee.
- **The Drawing Room:** A large wooden drafting table sits in the center of the room. Unrolled across it is **Drawing S-1: Foundation & Slurry Wall Excavation Plan**, held down at the corners by heavy steel rivets.
- **Interactive Provenance Moment:** You approach the drafting table and touch the drawing sheet. A clean, subtle parchment overlay appears:
  > **Drawing S-1 (Contract NYA-110.001)**  
  > *Approved for Construction: June 1966 — Chief Engineer, Port Authority of NY & NJ*  
  > *"Depicts 3,100-foot bentonite slurry wall perimeter trench designed by Icanda Ltd. to retain Hudson River water table during bedrock excavation."*
- **On the Walls:** Pinboards holding black-and-white site photographs, project milestone calendars showing **"Day 1: 1966"**, and early architectural renderings by Minoru Yamasaki showing the future 110-story towers.

---

### Minute 10: Standing at the Edge of the Slurry Wall Trench
- **Where You Stand:** You exit the site trailer and walk out onto the timber cat-walk cantilevered directly over the active slurry wall trench along West Street.
- **What You See:** Deep beneath you, a heavy steel clamshell bucket grabber drops 70 feet down into a narrow trench filled with gray, slurry liquid. Heavy drill rigs bore into bedrock to anchor tieback rods.
- **The Landmark Alignment:** Looking east across the open pit, you see the historic Woolworth Building, the Singer Building, and the 19th-century downtown skyline framed behind the rising dust of excavation.
- **The Temporal Discovery:** A small wooden chronological signpost beside you reads:
  > **"TODAY IS DAY 1 (1966). RETURN TOMORROW TO WITNESS DAY 2 (1967) — BEDROCK EXCAVATION AND FOUNDATION STEEL PLACEMENT."**

## 3. WHAT MAKES THIS ARRIVAL MEMORABLE

1. **Immediate Sensory Immersion:** The visitor is not greeted by buttons or database tables; they are greeted by 1966 New York City.
2. **The Power of Absence:** Standing on the empty site in 1966 makes the eventual rise of the towers awe-inspiring when the visitor returns on future days.
3. **Seamless Provenance:** The drawing room introduces technical evidence naturally as part of the physical world rather than as an enterprise interface.
4. **Living Chronology:** The visitor immediately understands the core law of the world: **time moves forward, and history is explorable.**
