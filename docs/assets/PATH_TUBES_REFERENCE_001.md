# PATH / Hudson Tubes
**Reference Document: Historic WTC Assets**

## Evidence
Photographs from the NYC Municipal Archives (1966-1968) and Port Authority engineering logs document the decision to keep the original Hudson & Manhattan (H&M) commuter rail lines active during the excavation. As the earth was dug away, the original 1909 cast-iron train tubes were exposed and left suspended in mid-air above the 70-foot deep pit.

## Dimensions
*   **Tube Diameter:** Approximately 15 to 17 feet outer diameter.
*   **Length:** Spanning hundreds of feet diagonally across the site from Church Street down toward the river.
*   **Support Truss Height:** Extending from the underside of the tubes all the way down to the bedrock floor (up to 70 feet long).

## Appearance
The tubes were composed of bolted cast-iron rings, giving them a heavily ribbed, segmented appearance. They did not float; they were propped up from below by massive, heavy-duty steel support towers featuring dense X-bracing to prevent shifting while trains rumbled through them. 

## Colors
*   **Tubes:** Dark, rusted iron black and deep brown, heavily weathered from decades underground.
*   **Supports:** Industrial grey/black steel, occasionally marked with safety orange or white stripes near the base to prevent machinery collisions.

## Placement
Cutting diagonally across the main excavation pit (the "Bathtub"). They formed a physical barrier traversing the site, requiring construction equipment to drive around or under the support trusses.

## Importance
This is arguably the most surreal and unique visual of the entire WTC construction. It visually communicates the complexity of building a mega-project in an active, dense urban environment.

## Implementation Notes
Do not use a smooth cylinder. The geometry must feature distinct ribbing/flanges at regular intervals to read as bolted cast-iron segments. The support trusses must look incredibly robust—thick I-beams with X-bracing, resting on wide concrete footings on the pit floor.
