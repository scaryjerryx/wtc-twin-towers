# PANYNJ Perimeter Hoarding
**Reference Document: Historic WTC Assets**

## Evidence
Photographs from the official groundbreaking ceremony on August 5, 1966, show the site perimeter fenced off to separate the active public sidewalks from the heavy excavation work. 

## Dimensions
*   **Height:** 8 to 10 feet tall.
*   **Length:** Continuous panels wrapping the entire accessible street-level perimeter of the 16-acre site.
*   **Main Signboards:** Massive custom billboards, roughly 12 feet high by 24 feet wide, erected at key corners (like Church & Cortlandt).

## Appearance
*   **Street-Facing:** Flat, continuous plywood sheets tightly fitted together. Public viewing "portholes" (small square cutouts covered with wire mesh) were periodically cut into the wood to let pedestrians watch the digging.
*   **Site-Facing:** Exposed timber framing. Diagonal 2x4 or 4x4 bracing anchored into the dirt to keep the walls from blowing over in the wind.
*   **Signage:** Clean, authoritative, bureaucratic. Large flat signboards bearing the Port Authority seal, the names of the architects (Minoru Yamasaki, Emery Roth & Sons), and the general contractor (Tishman Realty & Construction).

## Colors
*   **Wood:** Weathered raw plywood (pale yellow/brown) or painted solid Port Authority industrial blue.
*   **Signage:** Bright white backgrounds with stark, bold blue sans-serif typography (Helvetica or similar mid-century modernist fonts).

## Placement
Running exactly along the boundary line between the intact city sidewalks and the sudden drop-off of the excavation pit. 

## Importance
Establishes the bureaucratic and federal scale of the project. The contrast between the clean, modern typography on the fence and the chaotic, muddy destruction inside the fence is a key visual theme of 1966.

## Implementation Notes
Do not use simple chainlink or generic modern construction barriers (no orange plastic netting). The fence must be solid timber panels. The back side (visible to the player when inside the site) must show the repetitive diagonal structural bracing. Ensure the Yamasaki/PANYNJ project signs are highly legible.
