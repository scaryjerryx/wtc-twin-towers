# The "Bathtub" Retaining Wall
**Reference Document: Historic WTC Assets**

## Evidence
Documented extensively in Engineering News-Record (1966-1967) and visible today in the 9/11 Memorial Museum. The slurry wall was a 3,100-foot continuous concrete perimeter designed to hold back the Hudson River.

## Dimensions
*   **Depth:** Dropping 70 feet from street level down to Manhattan schist bedrock.
*   **Thickness:** 3 feet thick solid reinforced concrete.
*   **Panel Width:** Constructed in discrete 22-foot wide panels.

## Appearance
Unlike a standard sloped dirt excavation, the Bathtub edge was a sheer, vertical cliff of concrete.
*   **Panel Seams:** Distinct vertical lines separating each 22-foot pour.
*   **Tie-Back Anchors:** To prevent the wall from bowing inward under the pressure of the river, deep steel cables were drilled diagonally through the wall and into the bedrock outside the site. On the visible interior face, these tie-backs appeared as large circular steel caps/plates protruding from the concrete at regular grid intervals.
*   **Surface:** Rough, unfinished concrete cast directly against the earth trench. 

## Colors
*   **Concrete:** Mid-to-dark grey, heavily stained with streaks of black mud, rust, and white efflorescence from seeping groundwater.
*   **Tie-Backs:** Heavy, rusted dark brown/orange iron caps.

## Placement
Forming the absolute outer boundary of the deep excavation pit.

## Importance
The single most important structural element of the WTC complex. Without it, the site would be underwater. Visually, it transforms the "pit" from a mere hole in the ground into an intentional, highly engineered mega-structure.

## Implementation Notes
Replace the smooth or dirt-textured sides of the excavation pit with a segmented concrete wall texture. Model the tie-back anchor caps as physical geometry (small cylinders protruding from the wall at downward angles) arranged in horizontal rows to give the wall scale and immense structural weight.
