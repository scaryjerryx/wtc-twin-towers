# Shot Blocking Plan: SHOT 009
**Target: Excavation Floor Looking Up**
**Reference Document: SHOT_009_BLOCKING_PLAN_001**

## Objective
To establish the geometric composition required to communicate "a massive, man-made canyon" using only primitive wireframes and spatial relationships, before any lighting, materials, or textures are applied.

---

## 1. Foreground
**Object:** Bedrock Floor & Survey Markings
*   **Distance from camera:** 0 to 5 meters.
*   **Relative size in frame:** Bottom 15% of the frame.
*   **Importance:** High. Anchors the viewer to the bottom of the pit and establishes the raw, unrefined starting point of the canyon.

**Object:** Crawler Crane Tracks (Base)
*   **Distance from camera:** 5 to 10 meters (Placed immediately in front of the camera, slightly off-center).
*   **Relative size in frame:** Lower left or right quadrant.
*   **Importance:** Critical. Forces the viewer to immediately measure the rest of the scene against a massive piece of industrial machinery.

## 2. Midground
**Object:** Crawler Crane Lattice Boom
*   **Distance from camera:** 10 to 20 meters (Angled upward from the chassis).
*   **Relative size in frame:** Slicing diagonally across the middle 30% of the frame.
*   **Importance:** High. The boom acts as a massive "pointer" directing the viewer's eye upward along the vertical wall, emphasizing height.

**Object:** Lower Slurry Wall (First 30 feet)
*   **Distance from camera:** 15 to 25 meters (Filling the horizontal plane in front of the camera).
*   **Relative size in frame:** Dominates the central 40% of the frame.
*   **Importance:** Critical. The sheer verticality begins here, establishing the brutalist concrete face.

## 3. Background
**Object:** Upper Slurry Wall (Top 40 feet)
*   **Distance from camera:** 25 to 50 meters (As the wall races upward and away from the tilted camera).
*   **Relative size in frame:** Dominates the upper 35% of the frame.
*   **Importance:** Critical. The continuation of the wall geometry into the distance creates the claustrophobic "canyon" effect.

## 4. Wall & Tie-Back Visibility
**Object:** Tie-Back Anchor Grid
*   **Distance from camera:** 15 meters (bottom row) to 50 meters (top row).
*   **Relative size in frame:** Repeated geometric protrusions scaling smaller as they ascend the wall.
*   **Importance:** High. The grid provides a mathematical measuring stick. As the tie-backs recede and shrink toward the top rim, they force perspective and exaggerate the height of the wall.

## 5. Skyline & Rim Visibility
**Object:** Street-Level Timber Hoarding
*   **Distance from camera:** 60+ meters (At the very top edge of the wall).
*   **Relative size in frame:** A tiny, thin strip separating the concrete wall from the sky.
*   **Importance:** Critical. This is the ultimate scale reference. Seeing an 8-foot fence rendered as a tiny sliver at the top of the frame proves the 70-foot depth to the viewer's brain.

**Object:** The Sky
*   **Distance from camera:** Infinite.
*   **Relative size in frame:** Top 10% of the frame.
*   **Importance:** Moderate. Provides the claustrophobic release point; the "lid" of the canyon.

## 6. Scale Reference Placement Summary
1.  **Near-Frame Anchor:** The crawler crane base at `Y=-20`, 5 meters from the lens.
2.  **Mid-Frame Guide:** The lattice boom slicing upward.
3.  **Far-Frame Anchor:** The timber hoarding at `Y=0`, 60 meters away at the top rim.

*By locking these three objects into the frame simultaneously, the viewer's brain is forced to calculate the massive distance between the 100-ton crane at the bottom and the tiny pedestrian fence at the top, achieving the success criterion regardless of lighting or textures.*
