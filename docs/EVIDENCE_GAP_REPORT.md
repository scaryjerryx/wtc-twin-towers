## Gap Ranking System

| Rank | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Reconstruction cannot proceed without this evidence. Must be acquired before any spatial modelling of the affected area. |

## Critical Gaps

### CG-1: Tower B (South Tower) Structural Drawings

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Structural engineering sheets equivalent to AA20a1 for Tower B (WTC 2) |
| **Why required** | Tower B had a different structural system from Tower A (different column layout, different mechanical floors, different height). The AA20a1 collection covers Tower A only. Without Tower B structural drawings, only one tower can be reconstructed. |
| **Likely repositories** | Internet Archive, NIST FOIA releases, structural engineering archives (Leslie E. Robertson Associates / LERA), Skilling Helle Christiansen Robertson archives |
| **Likely file types** | PNG/TIFF scans of structural sheets, PDF drawing sets, DJVU documents |
| **Search terms** | "WTC 2 structural drawings", "South Tower structural sheets", "WTC Tower B framing plans", "AA20" (sister collection to AA20a1) |
| **Current status** | ❌ Not in corpus |

### CG-2: Architectural Floor Plans (All Buildings)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Architectural floor plans showing walls, rooms, corridors, elevators, stairs, and spatial layout for each floor of each building |
| **Why required** | Structural drawings show framing only — beams, columns, and floor decks. Architectural floor plans are required to understand the actual spatial organization: where rooms were, how corridors connected, where elevator banks and stairwells were located, and how tenant spaces were arranged. Without floor plans, the reconstruction is a structural skeleton with no interior spaces. |
| **Likely repositories** | Port Authority archives, Minoru Yamasaki Associates archives, Emery Roth & Sons archives, NIST investigation records, NYC Department of Buildings records |
| **Likely file types** | PDF drawing sets, TIFF scans, DWG files |
| **Search terms** | "WTC floor plan", "World Trade Center architectural drawings", "WTC tenant layout", "WTC core plan", "Yamasaki WTC plans" |
| **Current status** | ❌ Not in corpus |

### CG-3: Site Plan and Plaza Documentation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Site-level plans showing the relationship between all buildings, the Austin J. Tobin Plaza, underground concourse, street-level entrances, and surrounding infrastructure |
| **Why required** | The WTC complex was an integrated site — the buildings, plaza, concourse, and transportation infrastructure formed a connected system. Without site plans, the spatial relationship between buildings cannot be established, and the plaza (a defining feature of the complex) cannot be reconstructed. |
| **Likely repositories** | Port Authority archives, NYC Department of City Planning, NIST investigation records, Yamasaki archives |
| **Likely file types** | PDF site plans, TIFF scans, CAD files |
| **Search terms** | "WTC site plan", "World Trade Center plaza plan", "Austin Tobin Plaza drawings", "WTC concourse level plan" |
| **Current status** | ❌ Not in corpus |

### CG-4: Tower A Exterior Wall — Upper Floors

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Exterior wall schedules or drawings for Tower A floors 10-110 |
| **Why required** | The existing exterior wall schedule (XLS) covers only floors 1-9. The exterior wall panel system changed at mechanical floors and varied by elevation. Upper floor exterior wall data is required to model the full tower envelope. |
| **Likely repositories** | Same as CG-1 (structural drawing archives), NIST investigation records |
| **Likely file types** | Excel spreadsheets, PDF schedules, structural sheet scans |
| **Search terms** | "WTC exterior wall schedule", "Tower A wall panels floors 10-110", "WTC column cover drawings" |
| **Current status** | ⚠️ Partial (floors 1-9 only) |

## Important Gaps

### IG-1: Construction Photographs

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Photographs documenting the construction process (1966-1973) |
| **Why required** | Construction photographs provide visual confirmation of structural systems as-built, construction sequencing, material types, and construction methods. They bridge the gap between design drawings and as-built reality. They are also essential for the "living reconstruction" concept — showing the towers being built over time. |
| **Likely repositories** | Port Authority archives, Library of Congress (HABS/HAER), construction company archives (Tishman Realty & Construction), historical photograph collections, newspaper archives |
| **Likely file types** | JPEG, TIFF, PNG photographs |
| **Search terms** | "WTC construction photos", "World Trade Center under construction", "WTC steel erection", "Tishman WTC construction" |
| **Current status** | ❌ Not in corpus |

### IG-2: Interior Photographs

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Photographs of completed interiors — lobbies, elevator banks, corridors, tenant spaces, observation deck, Windows on the World restaurant, mechanical spaces |
| **Why required** | Interior photographs are the primary evidence for interior spatial reconstruction. They document finishes, materials, lighting, signage, furnishings, and spatial character. Without them, interior spaces can only be inferred from floor plans. |
| **Likely repositories** | Historical photograph collections, tenant archives, Port Authority promotional materials, tourist photographs, architectural photography archives |
| **Likely file types** | JPEG, TIFF photographs |
| **Search terms** | "WTC lobby photos", "Windows on the World interior", "WTC observation deck photos", "World Trade Center interior 1970s" |
| **Current status** | ❌ Not in corpus |

### IG-3: Buildings 3-7 Structural and Architectural Documentation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Structural drawings and architectural plans for WTC 3 (Marriott Hotel), WTC 4 (South Plaza Building), WTC 5 (North Plaza Building), WTC 6 (Customs House), and WTC 7 |
| **Why required** | The WTC complex included 7 buildings. The current corpus covers Tower A structural only, plus WTC7 specifications. Buildings 3-6 are completely undocumented. The reconstruction vision includes the full complex, not just the Twin Towers. |
| **Likely repositories** | Port Authority archives, NIST investigation records (WTC 7 was extensively studied), individual building architect archives |
| **Likely file types** | PDF drawings, TIFF scans, structural sheets |
| **Search terms** | "WTC 3 Marriott plans", "WTC 4 drawings", "WTC 5 plans", "WTC 6 Customs House", "WTC 7 structural drawings" |
| **Current status** | ⚠️ Partial (WTC7 specs only) |

### IG-4: Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing (MEP) Drawings

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | MEP drawings for all buildings — HVAC systems, electrical distribution, plumbing risers, fire protection systems |
| **Why required** | MEP systems define the operational character of the buildings. Mechanical floors (7-8, 41-42, 75-76, 108-109 for Tower A) are structurally distinct and visually prominent. Understanding MEP routing informs spatial constraints and mechanical floor modelling. |
| **Likely repositories** | Port Authority engineering archives, mechanical contractor archives, NIST investigation records |
| **Likely file types** | PDF MEP drawings, TIFF scans |
| **Search terms** | "WTC mechanical drawings", "WTC HVAC plans", "WTC mechanical floors", "WTC plumbing risers" |
| **Current status** | ❌ Not in corpus |

### IG-5: Architectural Elevations and Sections

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Architectural elevation drawings and building sections showing the exterior appearance, window patterns, lobby design, and vertical organization |
| **Why required** | The exterior appearance of the towers — the distinctive narrow windows, aluminum cladding, and column covers — is defined by architectural elevations. Building sections show the vertical relationship between floors, mechanical levels, and the lobby/plaza interface. |
| **Likely repositories** | Yamasaki archives, architectural photography collections, Port Authority promotional materials |
| **Likely file types** | PDF drawings, TIFF scans, architectural renderings |
| **Search terms** | "WTC elevation drawings", "Yamasaki WTC sections", "World Trade Center facade details" |
| **Current status** | ❌ Not in corpus |

### IG-6: Foundation and Subgrade Documentation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Evidence required** | Foundation engineering drawings, slurry wall details, basement-level plans, PATH station integration, "bathtub" construction documentation |
| **Why required** | The WTC foundation was a major engineering achievement — the slurry wall "bathtub" method allowed construction below the water table. The foundation defines the site's relationship to the Hudson River, the PATH train system, and the underground concourse. |
| **Likely repositories** | Port Authority engineering archives, geotechnical engineering archives, NIST investigation records |
| **Likely file types** | PDF engineering drawings, TIFF scans, geotechnical reports |
| **Search terms** | "WTC slurry wall", "WTC bathtub foundation", "WTC basement levels", "WTC PATH station drawings" |
| **Current status** | ❌ Not in corpus |
