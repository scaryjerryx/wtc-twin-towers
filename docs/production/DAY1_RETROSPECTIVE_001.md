# DAY 1 RETROSPECTIVE

## What Worked
- **Autonomous Multi-Agent Workflow**: The separation of concerns between Team A (Implementation), Team B (Research), Team C (Production Manager), and Team D (Critic) proved highly effective. It ensured historical accuracy drove technical implementation, which was strictly held to a high standard by the critic.
- **Narrative Over Geometry**: The strategy of focusing on the "visual contrast" (e.g., Radio Row vs. the massive pit) created much higher emotional resonance than simply detailing individual assets.

## What Failed
- **Initial Technical Ambition**: Early attempts to build custom GLSL shaders (e.g., Chroma Key avatars) caused blockers. Moving to standard R3F/Drei components (Billboards) restored velocity.
- **Render Quotas**: High-speed iterative realism passes exhausted the image generation engine's quota, halting visual review.

## Lessons Learned
- **Strict Escalation Policies**: Notifying the Project Director *only* at Milestone Reviews or Technical Blockers vastly improved production speed and reduced noise.
- **Score Calibration**: Capping scores at <10 unless museum-grade forced Team A to constantly push for better atmospheric depth and PBR material usage.

## Process Improvements
- Established a clean repository directory structure early (`/docs/production`, `/docs/research`, `/media/screenshots/shotXXX`).
- Instituted the mandatory Postmortem rule to capture reusable systems for future shots.

## Major Breakthroughs
- The "Dollhouse Effect" in SHOT004.
- The use of physical transmission materials to simulate bentonite slurry fluid in SHOT003.

## Recommendations for Day 2
- **Pre-fetch Assets**: Team B should queue research for Day 2 shots well before Team A finishes Day 1.
- **Resource Management**: Batch image generation requests to preserve API quotas.
