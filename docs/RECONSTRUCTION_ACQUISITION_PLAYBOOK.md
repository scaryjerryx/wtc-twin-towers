## 1. Critical Gap Acquisition Guides

### CG-1: Tower B (South Tower) Structural Drawings

#### What Is Needed

Structural engineering drawing sheets for WTC 2 (South Tower) equivalent to the AA20a1 Tower A collection already held. This includes:

- Column layout and framing plans for all floors
- Beam schedules and connection details
- Mechanical floor structural details (floors 7-8, 41-42, 75-76, 108-109)
- Foundation-to-tower transition details
- Hat truss and antenna base structural details (roof)

#### Why It Is Needed

Tower B had a different structural configuration from Tower A: different column grid, different mechanical floor locations, different overall height (1,362 ft vs 1,368 ft), different antenna structure, and different lobby configuration. Without Tower B structural drawings, the South Tower cannot be reconstructed at any spatial level.

#### Reconstruction Value: **Critical**

Without this evidence, reconstruction is limited to Tower A only. The Twin Towers concept requires both.

#### Acquisition Difficulty: **Moderate**

The AA20a1 Tower A collection came from Internet Archive. The sister collection for Tower B likely exists under a similar identifier. Internet Archive has a known, stable structure and the collection is public domain or government-produced.

### CG-2: Architectural Floor Plans (All Buildings)

#### What Is Needed

Architectural floor plans for every floor of every WTC building showing:

- Core layout (elevator banks, stairwells, mechanical shafts, restrooms)
- Tenant space boundaries and corridor configurations
- Lobby and entrance configurations (concourse level, plaza level, street level)
- Mechanical floor layouts (equipment rooms, louvers, access)
- Roof-level plans (cooling towers, mechanical penthouses, hat truss enclosure)
- Typical floor plans for each tower zone (low-rise, mid-rise, high-rise)

#### Why It Is Needed

Structural drawings show the skeleton — beams, columns, and floor decks. Architectural floor plans show the actual spatial organization: where people moved, worked, and gathered. This is the difference between a structural wireframe and a walkable reconstruction. Every Space, Zone, and Floor in the spatial hierarchy requires architectural plans.

#### Reconstruction Value: **Critical**

Without architectural floor plans, the reconstruction is a structural skeleton. Interior spaces — the actual experience of being in the World Trade Center — cannot be modelled.

#### Acquisition Difficulty: **High**

Original architectural drawings are held by the Port Authority, Minoru Yamasaki Associates, and Emery Roth & Sons. Many have not been digitized. The best publicly available source is the NIST NCSTAR report series, which reproduces selected floor plans as figures within investigation reports.

### CG-3: Site Plan and Plaza Documentation

#### What Is Needed

Site-level documentation showing the spatial relationship between all seven WTC buildings, the Austin J. Tobin Plaza, the underground concourse, street-level entrances, and surrounding infrastructure (West Street, Church Street, Vesey Street, Liberty Street, PATH station, subway connections).

Specifically:
- Overall site plan showing building footprints and plaza layout
- Plaza-level plan (elevation +0) showing the sculpture, planting, seating, and circulation
- Concourse-level plan (elevation -1) showing retail, PATH station, and building connections
- Basement-level plans (B1-B6) showing parking, mechanical, and service areas
- Street-level entrances and connections to surrounding streets
- The "bathtub" slurry wall perimeter

#### Why It Is Needed

The WTC complex was an integrated site — not seven separate buildings. The plaza was the defining public space, larger than St. Mark's Square in Venice. The concourse was a major retail and transportation hub. Without site-level plans, the spatial relationships between buildings cannot be established, and the plaza and concourse (essential to the visitor experience) cannot be reconstructed.

#### Reconstruction Value: **Critical**

The Site level is the foundation of the spatial hierarchy. Without it, buildings float in isolation with no relationship to each other or their context.

#### Acquisition Difficulty: **Moderate**

Site plans and plaza documentation exist in multiple public sources. The NIST reports document the site extensively. Port Authority promotional materials often included site diagrams. The plaza was well-photographed.

**Exact Repository Targets:**

### CG-4: Tower A Exterior Wall Schedules — Upper Floors

#### What Is Needed

Exterior wall panel schedules for Tower A floors 10-110, equivalent to the existing floors 1-9 Excel spreadsheet. This includes:

- Column cover panel types and dimensions
- Window module dimensions
- Spandrel panel types
- Mechanical floor louver configurations
- Corner panel details
- Panel type transitions at mechanical floors (7-8, 41-42, 75-76, 108-109)

#### Why It Is Needed

The existing spreadsheet covers only floors 1-9. The exterior wall system for the Twin Towers was a defining architectural feature — the narrow windows and aluminum cladding created the towers' distinctive appearance. The panel system changed at mechanical floors. Without complete schedules, the upper 90% of the tower envelope cannot be accurately modelled.

#### Reconstruction Value: **Critical**

The tower exterior envelope is the most visually recognizable element of the World Trade Center. Incomplete wall data means an incomplete tower.

#### Acquisition Difficulty: **Moderate**

The existing spreadsheet (floors 1-9) demonstrates that this data was tabulated in spreadsheet form. Upper-floor schedules almost certainly exist in the same format from the same source. The LERA/Skilling structural engineering archives are the most likely source.

## 6. Acquisition Strategy Summary

### Immediate Manual Acquisition (Operator-Directed)

The operator should manually search and download from these exact URLs:

1. **Internet Archive — AA20a1 related items**
   - Visit: `https://archive.org/details/AA20a1`
   - Click the uploader name to see all their collections
   - Look for: AA20, AA20b, "WTC 2", "South Tower", "Tower B" collections
   - Download any related structural sheet ZIPs

2. **NIST NCSTAR Reports**
   - Visit: `https://www.nist.gov/el/fire-research-division-73300/world-trade-center-investigation/NCSTAR-reports`
   - Download NCSTAR 1-1 through 1-9 (all appendices)
   - These contain reproduced floor plans, structural details, site diagrams, and exterior wall analysis

3. **Library of Congress — Gottscho-Schleisner Collection**
   - Visit: `https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/gsc/`
   - Search for "World Trade Center", "WTC"
   - This collection contains construction and completed-building photographs by professional architectural photographers

4. **Wikimedia Commons — WTC Construction**
   - Search: `https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?search=World+Trade+Center+construction`
   - Filter by: Images, by date (oldest first)
   - Already supported by existing acquisition pipeline
