# SHOT005 REFERENCE (PUBLIC OBSERVATION DECK)

**Source:** Team B (Research)

## Overview
During the massive excavation of the World Trade Center "Bathtub," the Port Authority erected public observation decks and viewing windows along the perimeter fencing (often Church Street). These allowed New Yorkers to watch the historic engineering project unfold while keeping them safe from the active 70-ft deep construction pit.

## Key Visual Elements (August 1966)
1. **Public Observation Areas**: 
   - Elevated wooden platforms or designated safe zones at street level.
   - Plywood hoarding fences (often painted blue or featuring Port Authority project info).
   - Cut-out windows (sometimes with wire mesh or plexiglass) at varying heights for adults and children.

2. **Public Access Conditions**:
   - Safely separated from the heavy machinery.
   - Pedestrians in 1960s summer attire (suits, dresses, hats).
   - Informational signage explaining the Slurry Wall or PATH tube underpinning.

3. **Scale and Visibility**:
   - Looking *down* into the 70-foot deep pit from street level.
   - The immense scale of crawler cranes and the suspended PATH tubes seen from a human perspective on the sidewalk.
