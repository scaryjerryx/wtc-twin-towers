# SHOT004 REFERENCE (RADIO ROW DEMOLITION EDGE)

**Source:** Team B (Research)

## Overview
Radio Row was a bustling neighborhood on the lower west side of Manhattan, famous for its dense concentration of electronic stores, radio repair shops, and hardware vendors. In 1966, this vibrant 13-block area was condemned and systematically demolished to make way for the massive World Trade Center "Bathtub" excavation.

## Key Visual Elements (August 1966)
1. **Surviving Storefronts**: 
   - Multi-story brick and masonry tenement buildings.
   - Ground floor storefronts packed with goods (radios, antennas, tubes).
   - Signage: Large overhanging signs, neon, awnings (e.g., "Cortlandt St", "Oscilloscopes", "TV Tubes").
   - Fire escapes on facades.

2. **Demolition Boundary**:
   - The sheer contrast between the dense urban fabric and the sudden, violent clearing.
   - Half-torn-down buildings exposing interior wallpapers and floor joists (the "dollhouse effect").
   - Debris piles: brick, timber, twisted metal.
   
3. **Construction Perimeter Conditions**:
   - Heavy timber hoarding fences painted with Port Authority colors.
   - Danger/Keep Out signs.
   - Dust and haze marking the active boundary.

4. **Visual Contrast**:
   - The small, human scale of the Mom-and-Pop shops versus the massive, industrial scale of the crawler cranes and slurry wall excavation encroaching upon them.
